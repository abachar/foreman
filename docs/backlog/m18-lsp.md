## Where it stands

**18.1 to 18.4 are landed** (2026-08-31). `cmd+click` and `ctrl+cmd+j` jump to a definition, errors are underlined and marked in the gutter, and the pointer at rest opens a popover with the diagnostic, the server's markdown, or both.

Two faults were found by checking against the real `sourcekit-lsp` rather than by the tests, and both were in the new plumbing rather than in the protocol:

- **`PipeIO`'s streaming closures inherited `@MainActor`** from the file's default isolation and are called on a `DispatchIO` queue: the first server started trapped in `swift_task_checkIsolated`. The extension is `nonisolated`.
- **`DispatchIO`'s low-water mark defaults to the whole requested length**, so a read of "everything" only calls back at EOF. `PipeIO.readToEnd` never noticed — it wants EOF — but a server would have delivered its first byte only once it died. `setLimit(lowWater: 1)`, and `PipeIOStreamingTests` now pins it against `/bin/cat`.

## Definition of done

Checked by hand in the running app, on the **four servers targeted first** (`03-study-lsp.md`, technical options), which are the author's actual projects: **Swift** (`xcrun sourcekit-lsp`, nothing to install), **React** (`typescript-language-server --stdio`), **Angular** (`ngserver`, on the `.html` templates) and **Java** (`jdtls`, with its per-project `-data`). The first three tasks are developed against sourcekit-lsp; the other three servers are met at the end of each task, not at the end of the milestone.

- **Swift** — I open a `.swift` of Foreman itself, I mistype a name: the line is underlined within a second, the gutter shows a dot, pointing at it gives the message. I fix it: everything disappears. Pointing at a symbol gives its signature and its doc comment, rendered like the markdown preview and without a network access. `cmd+click` opens the declaration at the right line; `ctrl+cmd+j` does the same from the keyboard.
- **React** — on a real `.tsx`: the same three, and a component imported from another file jumps correctly.
- **Angular** — `ngserver` declared on `.html`: a template error is underlined in the template. What is *not* expected, and is written down rather than fixed: the Angular part of an inline template in a `.ts`, which needs two servers on one extension (`questions.md`).
- **Java** — `jdtls` on a Maven or Gradle project, with `-data` pointing inside that project's `.foreman/`: it is the slowest to start, so it is what proves R39's `lsp.timeout` and its banner are usable — if the default of 10 s is wrong, this is where it shows.
- A definition in the standard library (Swift) or the JDK (Java) is refused with a banner saying where it is — and the author decides then whether R44 stays as it is (`questions.md`).
- I open a `.kt`, for which nothing is declared: nothing is underlined, `cmd+click` does nothing, one banner says why, the app does not stumble.
- I kill a server from the terminal: it comes back once; I kill it again: the language loses LSP with a banner, and typing is never blocked in either case.
- **Go to selector** — in an Angular template, `cmd+click` on a class defined in one of the project's stylesheets opens it on the right line; on a Tailwind class, nothing happens and nothing is said.
- Typing stays free of synchronous work — the M6 signpost budgets are unchanged.

## Decisions to take during the milestone

- **The popover: `NSPopover` or a SwiftUI overlay** (task 18.4). It has to follow the pointer, never steal the focus and not fight the text view's own tracking; the answer is whichever of the two does that with less code, decided by trying, not by arguing.
- **The four severity colours in both themes** (task 18.3): error and warning are obvious, `information` and `hint` are not — and a wavy underline in a light ground is not the same design problem as in a dark one (`design` R6).
- **Is one restart the right number** (R39, task 18.1)? A server that crashes on a file will crash again on it; a server killed by a rebuild deserves more than one. To be judged on what actually happens during the milestone.
- **Whether `didSave` is worth sending** (R37): the study makes it conditional on the server's sync options. If no server used in the milestone asks for it, the branch goes.
- **One match or a picker for a selector defined twice** (R48, task 18.5): a base sheet plus an override is the normal case in a real project, and "the first in workspace order" may well be the wrong one. To judge on the author's own CSS.
- **Out-of-root definitions** (R44, open in `questions.md`): the definition of done makes the author meet the case at least once, which is the point — the decision is taken from that, not before.
